## 1. Executive summary

This is the sixth defect analysis of the codebase, one day after the fifth. Between the previous report (`DEFECT_ANALYSIS-2026-09-19T21-51-46`, commit `e45eef2`) and this commit three pull requests landed: the fixes for that report's nine findings (#46), a benchmark re-measurement (#47) and a 48-item code-style round (#48) that touched 18 production files - the MDC partial-install rollback and best-effort restore moved from two copies into one shared pair of functions in `Mdc.kt`, the wiring report's observation line now takes the configured correlation header, member orders and visibilities changed, and the WebClient twin's two `Content-Length` folds became one helper. Every one of those refactors was diffed against `e45eef2` and found behaviour-preserving (section 2, "Carried items" and "Rejected candidates"); all nine carried findings are fixed and effective, in the sense that the mutation each of them pinned now goes red. The twins' lifecycle code - bounded body capture, exactly-once completion, fail-open guards, additive MDC and Reactor-context restoration, twin symmetry - was re-read in full and is sound.

**No Critical and no High finding.** Two Medium findings, both in the reactive twin's test suite: one test asserts the ORDER of two exchange lines that two threads emit with no happens-before between them (a synchronous `retry` runs the second attempt's body on a bounded-elastic worker before the first attempt's line is written), so it can go red on a loaded runner without a production defect; and the one thing a caller's cancel must do for the connector - forward `cancel()` upstream so the engine stops reading and releases the connection - is pinned by no test in either hand-written operator, because every cancel scenario uses a source that cannot show it (`Mono.never()`, `Flux.never()`, an already-emitted scalar, a deliberately inert publisher). Deleting the `upstream.cancel()` line leaves the whole suite green, at the module's most delicate spot.

The fifteen Low findings fall into four groups. Four production items at documented boundaries: the blocking twin opens its call scope OUTSIDE the `try` whose `catch (Throwable)` exists to keep the open-exchanges gauge truthful under an `Error`, so an `Error` from the host's MDC adapter at that one step leaves the gauge at +1 forever; the reactive operators hand the terminal signal on before their own bookkeeping without a `finally`, so a spec-violating subscriber that throws from `onComplete` does the same; a same-thread cancel that Reactor's `onOperatorError` issues from inside the delivery - an outer host filter that fails on the delivered response without reading it - is read as "the downstream took it" and the exchange stays open, a case the guide attributes to raw `exchange()` alone; and the ambient-context detection checks presence against the context's class loader but links against the module's, which fails a context start in a parent-first shared-lib deployment. One cache-invariant item in common: a tag-FOLDING meter filter returns one real meter for every host beyond the cap and the owner caches it under one entry per raw host, against the KDoc's "one entry per meter the registry holds". Six coverage gaps at documented contracts (the new shared rollback's `andThen` rule, the outcome-vocabulary guard, three of the tee's five guard sites, the connectors' single-buffer `Mono` path, the ADR-0010 scope layering order) plus one unbounded engine close. And four evidence-chain items outside the reactor: the consumer-smoke CI job's test summary runs from a subdirectory the script's glob cannot see, so it always reports "no reports found" on a green job; the benchmarks job's Maven cache keeps a stale `legatium-common` jar resolvable, so its API pin cannot fail after a forgotten version bump; the generated evidence and coverage texts still say "both modules" while three feed the numbers and only two reports are published; and the render benchmark's UTF-8 boundary exists only because both body sizes happen to be 1 mod 3.

**Test verdict.** *Reliability as a safety net:* high across all three modules - the score-5 suites go red under the mutation experiments applied here (the events counter hoisted above the level gate, the `NoopMeter` check deleted, the exactly-once CAS dropped, the late-arrival pass-through removed, the request sample re-gated on a response, the rollback dropped from the partial install, the wiring order swapped), fault injection is real (a JVM-globally armed failing MDC adapter, throwing counters and summaries including the fail-open counter itself, a `MeterFilter.map` hook that removes a meter from another thread mid-registration, a body that refuses a second subscription, a throwing masker at wiring), and the risky logic is checked deterministically with an injected clock and pinned ids. Two exceptions carry the report's Medium weight: one order assertion across threads (flaky by construction), and the cancel path of both reactive operators, where the suite is green with the propagation deleted. *Usage & coverage:* the pyramid is base-heavy - 426 unit and contract test methods plus 4 fuzz targets (540 Surefire test cases with the engine matrices) carry the behaviour; nine real-engine suites, Boot-builder and Brave tracing integrations and the consumer smoke test verify the seams; every wait that crosses a thread is bounded, the smoke test included since #46. *Most significant gaps and anomalies:*

- The reactive cancel propagation to the connector is unpinned in both operators - a regression would leak connections on every caller timeout with a green suite (finding 2).
- The retry-context test asserts an order two threads decide (finding 1).
- Six documented branches have no test that would catch their removal, four of them in code that changed in #48 or guards the reactive twin's optimised paths (findings 8-10, 12, 13).
- The consumer-smoke job's summary step cannot see its own reports, and the benchmarks job's API pin is defeated by the runner cache (findings 14, 15).

## 2. Scope & methodology

**Modules and roles.** `./legatium-common/` holds the byte-identical shared code the two twins inline through Shade (ADR-0003): the bounded buffer, properties and their origins, header selection and masking, activation, identity (`traceparent`, correlation header, counting id), classification, the metrics owner with the body-meter cache, the MDC scope and - new since `e45eef2` - the shared install/rollback/restore functions, timeout classification and the observation-wiring report. `./legatium-restclient-logging/` is the blocking twin (a `ClientHttpRequestInterceptor` whose emission point is `ClientHttpResponse.close()`), `./legatium-webclient-logging/` the reactive twin (an `ExchangeFilterFunction` with two hand-written Reactor operators whose emission point is the body publisher's terminal signal). `./consumer-smoke/` checks the shaded jars as a consumer sees them; `./benchmarks/` holds JMH measurements of the buffer and the body-meter cache.

**Stack detected.** Spring Boot 4.1.1 on Spring Framework 7.0.9, Micrometer 1.17.1; both blocking (`RestClient`/`RestTemplate`) and reactive (`WebClient`, Reactor) client stacks; no coroutines; virtual threads neither configured nor assumed (the one `synchronized` block in production code guards a `WeakHashMap` lookup at owner creation, never a blocking call); no persistence, messaging, scheduling or controllers - this is a library, whose entry points are the interceptor, the filter, two auto-configurations, three customizers and one `SmartInitializingSingleton` per twin. Optional dependencies: `spring-boot-restclient`/`spring-boot-webclient` (customizers), `io.micrometer:context-propagation` (ADR-0010 restoration).

**Test code in scope: yes.** The three modules carry 146, 148 and 141 declared test methods (by annotation: 138 `@Test` + 5 `@ParameterizedTest` + 3 `@FuzzTest`; 147 `@Test` + 1 `@FuzzTest`; 141 `@Test`), plus 3 in `consumer-smoke`; the blocking contract's 6 methods run in five engine suites and the reactive contract's 5 in four, so the local Surefire reports list 540 test cases. Test-quality heuristics were applied to every non-trivial test (evidential value by mutation thought experiment against the production code, determinism, isolation, risk-path coverage, sham concurrency, resource lifecycle, convention deviations against `./CONTRIBUTING.md`). The review was split across four parallel reviewers working from one brief; every finding they reported was re-verified by the author against the current code (the retry test's thread handover traced through `ObservedResponse.onError` and `Mono.retry`'s synchronous resubscription, the four cancel scenarios read for their sources, the call-scope statement order, the class-loader linkage, the effective POM, the summary script's glob, the benchmark arithmetic), and one candidate was rejected on that re-verification.

**Procedure.** Phase 1 ranked every production, test and build file (section 4). Phase 2 read every production file completely, concentrating on the code that changed since the previous report - `git diff e45eef2..HEAD` over 18 production files: the shared `installMdcEntries`/`restoreMdcEntries` and their two callers, `describe(correlationIdHeader)` and its two call sites, `BoundedBodyCapture.declaredLength` and its two call sites, the relocated `restoreQuietly`, the reordered `ClientLoggingMetrics` members, `@JvmStatic forRegistry`, the private `Wiring` - and re-read the twins' lifecycle for regressions. Every test file was read in full, with the recording fakes introduced in #48 (`ObservingExecution`, `ObservingExchange`, `WritingExchange`, `RecordingInterceptor`, `AwaitingLogger` in the caller's-context suite) checked for reads before writes, cross-thread reads and level-restoration order. A grep sweep over production code for `!!`, `lateinit`, ambient time sources, `Thread.sleep`, `synchronized`, `runBlocking`/`GlobalScope`, `catch (Throwable)` and `TODO`/`FIXME` found nothing beyond the documented sites (two `lateinit var upstream` in the Reactor subscribers, set in `onSubscribe`; two `catch (t: Throwable)` that close the gauge and rethrow; one `synchronized` at owner creation). Phase 3 checked each candidate against reachability, framework contracts (Reactive Streams §2.13, Reactor's `onOperatorError` cancel, JDK 21+ `HttpClient.close()`, Maven plugin-configuration merging, setup-java's Maven cache keys), ADRs, the module guide and the existing tests; style and taste items were discarded unless a documented convention was violated.

**No local verification took place**: no build, no test run, no benchmark. Finding 1 rests on the traced order of two statements and Reactor's synchronous resubscription, not on an observed flake; finding 3 on the order of `openCallScope` and the `try`; finding 15 on setup-java's documented cache keying, not on an observed CI run.
