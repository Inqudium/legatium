## 1. Executive summary

The codebase is in the same deliberate state the two September-16 reports described: bounded body capture, exactly-once completion, fail-open guards, additive MDC/context restoration and the twin symmetry are all present, the three findings of the last report are either fixed and pinned (mark/reset transparency, HEAD read state) or open by a recorded decision (meter re-resolution). Since then the buffer was rewritten (`BoundedByteBuffer`), a `rejected` outcome (ADR-0012), a named-client message and a DEBUG/TRACE wiring report were added; none of these introduced a defect this analysis could find.

One **High** production defect was found in the reactive twin, and it is new to this report. The body operator `ObservedBody` binds the exchange's terminal ownership to a *subscription*, not to the *body*: every `subscribe` creates a fresh observer with its own `ended` flag. Spring's `exchangeToMono`/`exchangeToFlux` subscribe to the body a second time after the handler consumed it (`releaseIfNotConsumed`, whose error Spring itself swallows), and Spring's `AbstractClientHttpResponse` - the base of the JDK, Jetty and HttpComponents connectors - answers that second subscription synchronously with `IllegalStateException("The client response body can only be consumed once")`. Because the first observer hands `onComplete` downstream *before* it completes the exchange, the second observer's error wins the exactly-once transition inside that very call: a healthy 200 with a fully read body is logged as `adapter_outcome=failure` at ERROR with the bogus cause, on every such call, deterministically. The default Reactor Netty connector is not affected (its second subscription completes empty), which is why the suite - Reactor Netty in every integration test, `retrieve()` in every connector suite, re-subscribable `Flux.just` bodies in every unit test - is green. The finding is traced through the framework sources line by line but was not executed; the reproduction recipe is in finding 1.

Everything else is Medium or Low: three coverage gaps at risk paths (the emitter's `freeze()` call, the blocking twin's MDC/bookkeeping fail-open guards, the header `excludes` precedence), and a tail of test-hygiene items (a port-keyed cumulative assertion shared across cached contexts, an unclosed JDK client, an unbounded `Future.get()`, a static shared peer, a fuzz oracle that can misfire, worker exceptions not surfaced) plus the deliberately open meter re-resolution pattern.

**Test verdict.** *Reliability as a safety net:* high for the blocking twin and the common module, whose risk paths are pinned by deterministic unit tests with real converters, real fault injection and real contention, and would go red under the mutation experiments applied here; high for the reactive twin's state machine, cancel heuristic, tee order and fail-open paths - with one blind spot that is exactly the High production finding: exactly-once emission is proven only against bodies that tolerate a second subscription, so the suite actively reassures where it should not. *Usage & coverage:* the pyramid is base-heavy and healthy - about 400 fast, injected-clock, no-sleep unit and contract tests carry the behaviour; nine real-engine suites (five request factories, four connectors), Boot builder and Brave tracing integrations and three fuzz targets in regression mode verify the seams; the only wall-clock dependence is in the timeout scenarios with a 7.5x margin. *Most significant gaps and anomalies:*

- No test runs `exchangeToMono`/`exchangeToFlux` against a single-subscriber body (a real JDK/Jetty/HttpComponents connector or a fake that refuses a second subscription); the one "exactly once" test releases a `Flux.just` body twice (finding 2).
- The reactive emitter's `freeze()` before emission has no pinning test; deleting it keeps the suite green (finding 3).
- The blocking twin's fail-open guards around the call-scope MDC, the caller-MDC snapshot, the emission teardown and the body-meter recording are asserted only through one restorer seam (finding 4).
- The header `excludes` rule has no deterministic unit test in the module that owns it (finding 5).
- The reactive twin's integration awaits (`awaitEvents(n)`) return as soon as `n` events exist, so a surplus event is invisible to them: on the reactive stack, exactly-once against real connectors is unit-only evidence (pattern P3). The blocking twin's suites assert `log.events.single()` after a synchronous close and are not affected.

## 2. Scope & methodology

**Modules and roles.** `./legatium-common/` holds the byte-identical shared code the two twins inline through Shade (ADR-0003): the bounded buffer, properties and their origins, header selection and masking, activation, identity (`traceparent`, correlation header, counting id), classification, the metrics owner, MDC scope, timeout classification and the observation-wiring report. `./legatium-restclient-logging/` is the blocking twin (a `ClientHttpRequestInterceptor` whose emission point is `ClientHttpResponse.close()`), `./legatium-webclient-logging/` the reactive twin (an `ExchangeFilterFunction` with two hand-written Reactor operators whose emission point is the body publisher's terminal signal). `./consumer-smoke/` checks the shaded jars as a consumer sees them; `./benchmarks/` holds JMH measurements of the buffer.

**Stack detected.** Spring Boot 4.1.1 on Spring Framework 7.0.9; both blocking (`RestClient`/`RestTemplate`, Spring MVC-agnostic) and reactive (`WebClient`, Reactor) client stacks; no coroutines; virtual threads neither configured nor assumed (the id generator explicitly avoids `ThreadLocal` for them); no persistence, messaging, scheduling or controllers - this is a library, whose entry points are the interceptor, the filter, two auto-configurations, three customizers and one `SmartInitializingSingleton` per twin. Optional dependencies: `spring-boot-restclient`/`spring-boot-webclient` (customizers), `io.micrometer:context-propagation` (ADR-0010 restoration).

**Test code in scope: yes.** The three modules carry 134, 136 and 133 test methods (plus 3 in `consumer-smoke`), counted by annotation; test-quality heuristics were applied to every non-trivial test (evidential value by mutation thought experiment against the production code, determinism, isolation, risk-path coverage, sham concurrency, resource lifecycle, convention deviations against `./CONTRIBUTING.md`). The test review was split by module across three parallel reviewers working from the same production-code reading; their findings were re-verified by the author before inclusion.

**Procedure.** Phase 1 ranked every production and test file (section 4). Phase 2 read every production file completely and followed identity, body bytes, read state, failure, cancellation, metrics and context across module and framework boundaries, starting with the score-5 files; the reactive lifecycle was traced through Spring's `DefaultWebClient`, `DefaultClientResponse`, `DefaultClientResponseBuilder` and the four connector response classes, Reactor Netty's `FluxReceive`, the JDK `PublishingBodySubscriber`, Jetty's `AbstractSinglePublisher`/`QueuedSinglePublisher` and HttpComponents' `ReactiveDataConsumer`, all from the versions the build resolves. Phase 3 checked each candidate against reachability, framework contracts, ADRs and the existing tests; style and taste items were discarded unless a documented convention was violated.

**No local verification took place**: no build, no test run, no benchmark. Finding 1 rests on a line-by-line execution sketch through the framework sources, not on an executed reproduction. Existing `./**/target/` output was not used as evidence for this run.

**Known blind spots.** Connector and engine behaviour outside the nine engine families the tests cover; third-party `ClientResponse`/`ClientHttpResponse` implementations that violate their contracts; performance magnitudes (no profiler run); pure security, release infrastructure and dependency advisories are outside this report.

### 🟠 High

- [x] 1. [./legatium-webclient-logging/src/main/kotlin/eu/inqudium/legatium/webclient/logging/ObservedBody.kt:64-67, :79, :123-139] {High} {Confidence: high} {reactive / correctness} A second subscription to the response body completes the exchange as `failure` on the JDK, Jetty and HttpComponents connectors - every `exchangeToMono`/`exchangeToFlux` whose handler consumed the body logs a healthy call at ERROR
  - Symptom → cause: `ObservedBody.subscribe` creates a new `Observer` per subscription, each with its own `ended` flag (line 79), and every observer's terminal signal calls `onTerminal` - the filter's exactly-once `complete`. Spring's `exchangeToMono` (spring-webflux 7.0.9, `DefaultWebClient.java:414-426`) chains `.flatMap(value -> releaseIfNotConsumed(response).thenReturn(value))`, and `releaseIfNotConsumed` (`:198-204`) is `response.releaseBody().onErrorComplete()`, where `releaseBody()` (`DefaultClientResponse.java:171-173`) subscribes to the body again; the mutated response's body is `other.bodyToFlux(DataBuffer.class)` (`DefaultClientResponseBuilder.java:101-106`), which reaches the connector's `getBody()` on each subscription. For the JDK, Jetty and HttpComponents connectors that is `AbstractClientHttpResponse.getBody()` (spring-web 7.0.9), whose `SingleSubscriberPublisher` answers any second subscription synchronously with `onSubscribe(NO_OP)` + `onError(IllegalStateException("The client response body can only be consumed once"))` - Spring swallows it with `onErrorComplete()`, this module does not. Execution sketch for `exchangeToMono { it.bodyToMono(String::class.java) }` on the JDK connector: the first observer's `onComplete` (line 133) marks the capture complete, then calls `actual.onComplete()` (line 136) BEFORE `onTerminal` (line 137); inside that call Spring's decoder joins the body, emits the string, `flatMap` subscribes `releaseBody()` synchronously, the second observer receives the `IllegalStateException`, sets `exchange.failure` (line 125), forwards it (swallowed downstream) and calls `onTerminal` (line 127), which wins the `RESPONDED → COMPLETED` transition (`ClientRequestLoggingFilter.kt:259-264`); the emitter classifies `failure != null` as `ClientOutcome.FAILURE` at ERROR with the exception as cause. The first observer's own `onTerminal` then finds the exchange completed. ### P3 — The reactive twin's integration awaits are blind to a surplus event

- **Occurrences:** every integration and connector suite of the reactive twin waits with `awaitEvents(n)` (`./legatium-webclient-logging/src/test/kotlin/eu/inqudium/legatium/webclient/logging/AwaitingAppender.kt:30-33` returns as soon as `n` permits exist), then asserts on `.single()` or `hasSize(n)` over the events present at that instant. The blocking twin is not affected: its emission is synchronous at `close()` on the calling thread, and its suites assert `log.events.single()` without waiting (`./legatium-restclient-logging/src/test/kotlin/eu/inqudium/legatium/restclient/logging/RequestFactoryContract.kt:125` and five more), which does catch a surplus line.
- **Counting basis:** the `awaitEvents` definition in `AwaitingAppender.kt` and its call sites in the reactive twin's `*IntegrationTest.kt` and `ConnectorContract.kt` files; the blocking twin's `RequestFactoryContract.kt` and `ClientRequestLoggingInterceptorIntegrationTest.kt` inspected for comparison.
- **Pattern:** a second line emitted a few milliseconds after the awaited one is invisible to the assertion, so on the reactive stack exactly-once emission against real connectors rests on the unit tests alone - which, per finding 2, run against re-subscribable fakes. Finding 1 would not have been caught by the reactive integration suites even on an affected connector unless the outcome itself were asserted.
- **Direction:** after the await, settle briefly (or await the exchange's gauge returning to zero) and assert the exact count, and assert `adapter_outcome` and the absence of a cause in the happy-path connector scenarios.
- **Status (2026-09-19):** the second half is taken - the new `exchangeToMono` scenario of `ConnectorContract` asserts level, `adapter_outcome=success` and the absence of a cause on every connector, which is what caught finding 1 on the JDK connector before the fix. The settle is not: a gauge-zero poll can observe zero before the event is appended (the gauge closes before the line is written), and a fixed delay per await would cost every reactive suite wall-clock for a surplus the unit tests now pin against a single-subscriber body. Open as a pattern, by decision.
